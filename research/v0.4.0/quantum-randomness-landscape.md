# Quantum, randomness, and proof-system choices for v0.4.0

Research cutoff: **September 1, 2026**. Prepared September 20, 2026. This is a roster proposal, not a certification of a new matrix. The companion [JSON dossier](quantum-randomness-landscape.json) records candidate definitions, sources, alternatives, and transfer limits. No change to the scored universe is assumed.

The current roster has the essential broad quantum endpoints—BQP, QCMA, and QMA—but leaves several established questions without their natural endpoints. The strongest additions in this review are **QMA(2), StoqMA, QSZK, and QPCP[O(1)]**. **BPL** is a high priority shared with the space review. **BQL** is a strong bridge between quantum computation and space complexity. **QMA₁** is a defensible alternative of comparable foundational interest; **NISZK** and **QIP(2)** support a different, interaction-focused allocation.

These are editorial judgments about scientific coverage. They are not a likelihood ranking, an expert poll, or an estimate of which statements an AI could prove. None is selected because its formalization looks easy. Selecting all of them would use too many of a 40–50-class budget; the alternatives below make that cost visible.

## What the present endpoints capture

Keep BQP, QCMA, and QMA. They distinguish efficient quantum computation, classical certificates checked quantumly, and quantum certificates. The difference between classical and quantum witnesses is a substantive question, not a notational variant. Gharibian's pre-cutoff survey treats QCMA, QMA₁, QMA(2), and StoqMA as separate conceptions of quantum verification, with their own motivations and obstacles. [Gharibian, *The 7 faces of quantum NP*, 2023 preprint, §§4–7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18010v1).

Keep BPP and meaningful representatives of zero-sided and one-sided error. P versus BPP remains the broad derandomization question; RP and ZPP expose distinctions that a lone BPP endpoint loses. Keep MA, AM, and SZK: verification order, public randomness, and statistical simulation are different resources. SBP becomes particularly useful if StoqMA is selected, since it connects that physical model to classical probabilistic verification. The existing counting endpoints should be judged jointly with the counting review, rather than removed merely to make the quantum list longer.

**coMA, coAM, and coQMA are plausible demotions to unscored background nodes.** They are not known aliases of MA, AM, and QMA. Demoting them sacrifices direct endpoint coverage and changes the weighting of complement-related results. Keeping their definitions and inference rules preserves useful reasoning vocabulary, but does not make the scored roster equivalent. The strongest argument for retaining them is precisely the importance of complement closure and comparisons involving complementary proof systems. My preference is to spend some of these slots on missing resources, provided the release makes that tradeoff explicit.

## Candidate questions and their exact scope

| Candidate | Scientific question gained | Recommendation and strongest counterargument |
|---|---|---|
| QMA(2) | Does unentanglement increase verification power? Is QMA(2) as large as NEXP? | High priority. Two unentangled proofs add a resource absent from every current quantum row. Counterargument: its standard formulation is a promise class, and restricted-proof progress often does not settle the broad class. |
| StoqMA | Can stoquastic verification be simulated by MA? | High priority. It joins Hamiltonian complexity, randomness, and verification. Counterargument: several important physical results concern subclasses or gap parameters, not a class equality. |
| QSZK | How much power does quantum statistical zero knowledge add? Is it contained in QMA or BQP? | High priority. It represents state distinguishability and simulation, rather than another witness restriction. Counterargument: some of its best motivations are promise problems and cryptographic consequences outside the matrix. |
| QPCP[O(1)] | Can every QMA proof be checked by inspecting constantly many proof qubits? | High priority in a promise track. This is an established class formulation of the quantum PCP conjecture. Counterargument: it concentrates one slot on a particular conjecture and requires careful reduction conventions. |
| BPL | Can randomness be removed in logarithmic space? | High priority across domains. P versus BPP does not answer L versus BPL. Counterargument: RL is also a reasonable representative; avoid crowding the roster with every logspace error convention. |
| BQL | Does logarithmic quantum space exceed probabilistic or deterministic logarithmic space? | Strong addition if space breadth is emphasized. Counterargument: many complete problems are promise problems, and one must count classical workspace too. |
| QMA₁ | Can quantum witnesses have perfect completeness? | Strong foundational alternative. Counterargument: exact gate conventions matter, and a single question must compete with entire missing resource families. |
| NISZK | Does statistical zero knowledge require interaction? | Strong classical alternative. Counterargument: the complete problems and equivalences normally use promises and a common random string. |
| QIP(2) | What is the power of two-message quantum interaction between QMA and PSPACE? | Strong interaction alternative. Counterargument: a quantum-heavy roster could spend this slot on a different field. |

The primary anchors are more informative than counting how often these names appear. Harrow–Montanaro connect QMA(2) to product-state testing and show that polynomially many unentangled witnesses give the same class as two, so QMA(3), QMA(4), and QMA(poly) do not each deserve a slot. [*Testing product states, quantum Merlin–Arthur games and tensor optimisation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0017). Separability testing also supplies natural complete problems for BQP, QMA, QMA(2), and QSZK; changing the distance measure can change the class. [Gutoski–Hayden–Milner–Wilde, 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.5788).

StoqMA's connection to sign-problem-free Hamiltonians is a reason to include it independently of quantum computing hardware. Its central error-reduction problem is still consequential: the cited work shows that the proposed amplification would give StoqMA = MA. [Aharonov–Grilo–Liu, *StoqMA vs. MA: the power of error reduction*, 2025 version, Conjectures 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02835v4). The May 2026 sparse-Hamiltonian completeness result adds a recent primary example of the same research program. [Grilo–Rozos, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02845v1).

QSZK has a distinct cryptographic connection: certain separations involving QSZK imply quantum commitment constructions under the paper's stated assumptions and security model. This supports its relevance without turning a cryptographic consequence into an unconditional separation. [Chailloux–Kerenidis–Rosgen, 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.2793). A June 2026 version of a new upper-bound paper still places QSZK in QIP(2) ∩ coQIP(2), while improving the honest prover's space; it does not establish QSZK ⊆ QMA or PP. [Le Gall–Liu–Wang, 2026, abstract and main theorems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11597v2).

The quantum PCP conjecture concerns the persistence of computational hardness under constant relative error in ground-state energy, and has a long CS–physics research history. [Aharonov–Arad–Vidick, 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7495). Its proof-checking version has an explicit class definition: use one polynomial-size quantum proof, polynomial-time verification, and a constant number of queried proof qubits. Then the missing inclusion is QMA ⊆ QPCP[O(1)]. The reduction to constant-gap local Hamiltonians can be quantum; replacing it with a classical Karp reduction would change the assertion. [Buhrman–Helsen–Weggemans, 2025, Definition 5 and §1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04841v3). A July 2026 Simons research talk on gap amplification provides an independent pre-cutoff indication of continuing activity. [Nguyen, July 23, 2026](https://simons.berkeley.edu/talks/quynh-t-nguyen-harvard-university-2026-07-23).

BPL has unusually direct support for a broad roster: Vadhan explicitly lists L = RL and L = BPL as open problems in a general pseudorandomness text. [*Pseudorandomness*, §2.4, Open Problem 2.47](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/pseudorandomness/). Quantum logspace has also produced total-language examples: Apers–Edenhofer give a natural BQL problem not known to be in L or BPL, rather than relying only on a promise-complete matrix problem. [CCC 2025, Theorem 4 and conclusion](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2025.18). The equivalence of the general and unitary space models is supported by an explicit measurement-elimination theorem, not by the usual time-only deferred-measurement argument. [Fefferman–Remscrim, STOC 2021, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03530).

For QMA₁, the pre-cutoff case already suffices: Quantum 3-SAT is complete for a gate-specified perfect-completeness class, and the exact gate issue is discussed in the definition. [Gosset–Nagaj, 2013, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.0290). NISZK has longstanding complete problems and a precise connection between complement closure and equality with SZK. [Goldreich–Sahai–Vadhan, 1999, Theorems 1.3, 1.5–1.6](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~/oded/PSX/gsv2.pdf). A later impossibility result concerns black-box reductions; it does not prove an ordinary separation. [Lovett–Zhang, 2016](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2016/118/). For QIP(2), use a verifier-to-prover message followed by a reply, with the prover applying an arbitrary quantum operation. Its PSPACE upper bound has an explicit model definition. [Jain–Upadhyay–Watrous, 2009, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.1300).

## Total languages and promise problems need separate recommendations

Write `Tot(C)` for the total decision languages obtained by requiring a verifier for the promise class C to satisfy its yes/no gap on **every** input. This is the current benchmark convention. The canonical quantum literature often instead studies disjoint pairs `(Yes, No)` and imposes no requirement outside their union. Watrous makes this convention explicit at the start of his survey. [*Quantum Computational Complexity*, 2008, §II.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.3401).

The following are mathematical transfer observations, not claims that every author uses the same notation:

1. A promise-class inclusion `C ⊆ D` gives `Tot(C) ⊆ Tot(D)`. A total-language separation gives the corresponding promise separation.
2. A promise separation need not supply a total-language witness. A total-language inclusion need not solve the promise-class inclusion. A verifier can have intermediate acceptance probabilities on inputs outside the promise, and arbitrarily assigning those inputs to a side need not preserve its gap.
3. There is a useful exception on the source side. Every promise problem in P, NP, PSPACE, or NEXP has a completion in the corresponding total class: use the machine's deterministic or existential acceptance language. Therefore `Tot(NEXP) ⊆ Tot(QMA(2))` implies the canonical promise inclusion `NEXP ⊆ QMA(2)`. Combined with the standard upper bound, the QMA(2) = NEXP question really is represented by that total pair.
4. That exception does not justify replacing the canonical QMA(2) versus QMA, QMA versus QCMA, or QMA versus QPCP questions with total versions and claiming complete coverage. Their source verifier gaps are semantic conditions.

The issue is not peculiar to quantum computation. Promise-BPP derandomization is stronger than the usual total-language statement without an additional completion argument; the connection to pseudodeterministic approximate probability estimation is explicit in the literature. [Dixon–Pavan–Vinodchandran, 2021, introduction and Theorem 3.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2021/043/).

**Constrained recommendation.** If this revision remains a total-language benchmark, add BPL and seriously consider BQL and QMA(2). StoqMA and QSZK still add coherent total-language comparisons. QMA₁ and QPCP remain valid possible nodes, but name their targets as total restrictions and disclose the incomplete coverage of their famous promise formulations. Do not claim that a canonical promise separation necessarily earns a point.

**Typed-track recommendation, contingent on permission.** Use an explicit promise-problem block for canonical QMA, QCMA, QMA₁, QMA(2), StoqMA, QSZK, QIP(2), and QPCP comparisons. Include the necessary classical promise anchors, rather than connecting a promise node directly to a total-language node with an untyped inclusion edge. Cross-track transfer theorems need their own types and provenance. A conversion of the entire old matrix to promises would require a new audit, including advice, intersections, unambiguity, and counting conventions. It is not a change of display labels.

## Competing allocations under the slot constraint

These are **domain allocations**, to be combined with the independent classical and counting proposals. They do not endorse cuts in fields this review did not assess. The common retained domain is eleven endpoints: `RP, coRP, ZPP, BPP, SBP, MA, AM, SZK, BQP, QCMA, QMA`. It demotes `coMA, coAM, coQMA` to background. BPL and BQL are counted here once even though the space review also considers them.

| Allocation | Additions to the eleven retained endpoints | Domain slots | Cost relative to the old 14-domain allocation |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Conservative total-language breadth | BPL, BQL, QMA(2), StoqMA | 15 | +1; leaves QSZK, quantum PCP, and perfect completeness without their own new endpoints |
| Broad proof and physics coverage | BPL, QMA(2), StoqMA, QSZK, QPCP[O(1)], QMA₁ | 17 | +3; strongest case with an authorized promise track; omits BQL |
| Interaction and space emphasis | BPL, BQL, QMA(2), QSZK, NISZK, QIP(2) | 17 | +3; omits stoquastic verification, QMA₁, and quantum PCP endpoints |
| Smaller cross-field compromise | BPL, QMA(2), StoqMA, QSZK | 15 | +1; reserves more slots for counting, circuits, algebraic, or search questions |

The last allocation is my preferred starting point if the overall roster must stay near 50 and the other domain reviews uncover comparably important omissions. The broad proof allocation is my preferred quantum/proof expansion if enough slots are available. This preference changes with the stated scientific emphasis; there is no evidence-based numerical weight that establishes a unique best roster.

A separate promise track has an additional accounting cost. For example, a minimal twelve-node block `prP, prBPP, prNP, prMA, prPSPACE, prNEXP, prBQP, prQCMA, prQMA, prQMA2, prStoqMA, prQSZK` already uses twelve scored endpoints if each typed class counts as a slot. Adding QPCP and QMA₁ makes fourteen. Some total quantum rows could be replaced, but duplicate promise and total nodes must not be hidden in a claim of fifty classes. Sampling would require yet another object type. The roster decision needs to state whether the approximate limit counts semantic families or typed endpoints; this proposal assumes the stricter endpoint count until changed.

## Candidates that should not win slots merely by being familiar

**Known equivalent models belong in background vocabulary.** PostBQP = PP and PreciseQMA = PSPACE supply powerful interpretations of existing endpoints. Adding their names as separately scored copies would mostly duplicate points. [Aaronson, 2004/2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0412187); [Fefferman–Lin, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01975). QCMA with perfect completeness likewise does not introduce a new class under the theorem's exact-gate assumptions. [Jordan–Kobayashi–Nagaj–Nishimura, 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.5306). QIP = PSPACE, and the broader interactive-proof landscape is useful explanatory material without requiring an extra synonymous endpoint. [Vidick–Watrous, *Quantum Proofs*, 2016, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.01664).

**Quantum advice is substantive but expensive in slots.** BQP/qpoly versus BQP/poly is a real distinction between quantum and classical advice. Advice depends on input length, not on the particular input. Known upper bounds connect it to classical advice classes. [Aaronson, 2004/2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0402095); [Aaronson–Drucker, 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0377). Capturing the direct question needs both endpoints, with explicit nonuniform semantics. A February 2026 classical-oracle separation strengthens motivation but supplies no ordinary separation. [Bostanci–Huang–Vaikuntanathan, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09385). I rank this pair behind several single-slot additions for a balanced roster, not because advice is unimportant.

**DQC1 is a serious alternative with a model-choice trap.** A one-clean-qubit device starts with one pure qubit and a maximally mixed remainder; unrestricted reinitialization would destroy the restriction. Shepherd's BQ1P uses logspace-uniform circuits whose gates can be controlled by input bits. P containment is not immediate in that model. [Shepherd, 2006, §§1, 3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0608132). A polynomial-time classical controller can itself decide P languages, which gives a different comparison baseline. Later work gives strong error-reduction and trace-estimation results for explicitly specified clean-qubit models. [Fujii et al., 2015/2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07276). This deserves a restricted-quantum-computation roster, but an unspecified “DQC1” label is not ready for this matrix.

EQP, ZQP, quantum circuit depth classes, unique-witness QMA, quantum polynomial hierarchies, PostBPP, and restricted unentangled-witness variants remain alternatives. They should earn slots through a named missing question and precise conventions. A randomized reduction to a unique-witness promise problem is not an ordinary class equality; a short-witness or vanishing-gap theorem is not a theorem about constant-gap QMA(2). The 2026 StoqMA(2) paper is an instructive example: its EXP bound belongs to that restricted class, while its introduction still identifies NEXP as the general QMA(2) upper bound. [Liu–Wu, July 2026 version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27886v2).

## Definition and implementation decisions to settle after selection

Scientific importance and implementation effort are separate. Selecting a difficult model is allowed; quietly substituting an easier model is not.

- **StoqMA:** specify the allowed reversible gates, zero and plus ancillas, X-basis output measurement, and efficiently describable completeness/soundness thresholds with inverse-polynomial separation. The conventional range has soundness at least 1/2. Ordinary QMA's 2/3-versus-1/3 template is inappropriate, and constant-gap equivalence cannot be assumed while amplification is open.
- **QMA(2):** require two independently supplied registers with a product witness; soundness quantifies over every allowed product witness. Convex mixtures and entanglement across repetitions require explicit treatment. Use an established amplification theorem before normalizing parameters.
- **QMA₁:** fix the exact gate set and input encoding. Approximate universality alone cannot preserve acceptance probability exactly one.
- **QPCP:** specify one proof, constant query count, adaptive-query semantics, uniformity, and fixed completeness/soundness; use a union over constant query bounds if intended. Distinguish proof-checking from local-Hamiltonian formulations and reduction strengths.
- **BPL/BQL:** count both runtime and workspace; the classical input is read-only and classical control is resource-bounded. Unitary-versus-general equivalence needs the space-efficient theorem.
- **QSZK/NISZK/QIP(2):** define malicious-verifier versus honest-verifier simulation, common-reference-string resources, message order, and classical descriptions of quantum inputs. A state supplied as input is a different problem universe from a circuit describing that state.

The companion JSON treats familiar inclusions as **anchors for a later baseline audit**, not as authorized seed changes. The selected models still require operational definitions, exact source matching, and a new historical classification review before freezing a release.

## Coverage limits and contamination

Decision-class pairs will miss important advances even with a better roster. Sampling, search, state preparation, quantitative speedups, pseudorandom generators, concrete cryptographic constructions, and oracle/query separations often do not imply a new ordinary pair label. Aaronson's sampling–search equivalence explicitly concerns those task types; it is not a theorem identifying decision BPP with BQP. [*The Equivalence of Sampling and Searching*, 2010/2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.5104). His quantum-query problem collection illustrates substantial research questions outside an ordinary inclusion matrix. [2021 collection](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/open.pdf).

Problem-specific derandomization is another limit. The classical review identified the pre-cutoff 2026 deterministic NC result for maximum-weight perfect bipartite matching as a historical coverage test. It is a major advance without an automatic collapse of the broad BPL, BPP, or NC pairs. This proposal does not promise to capture every comparably important future result.

Post-cutoff papers must be kept out of baseline eligibility and out of importance selection. Searches encountered September 2, 2026 work claiming a **quantum-oracle** QMA(2)/QMA separation, and September 11 and 14 papers claiming perfect completeness for QMA and related proof systems. Their validity was not audited here. Their dates and existence belong in a contamination advisory for future model evaluations, not in this cutoff's known-answer graph. The case for QMA₁ above rests on 2013 and 2023 sources. The JSON records the exact post-cutoff identifiers so these claims are not accidentally imported.

This review is substantial but bounded: it follows primary surveys and research chains, with targeted pre-cutoff updates. It does not claim a complete literature census or a human expert consensus. The next useful review is of the chosen definitions and the lost/gained named questions, followed by pairwise classification for the selected roster.
