# Quantum baseline extension for provisional v0.4.0

The four new endpoints are **BQL, QMA(2), StoqMA and QSZK**, interpreted as total binary decision languages. Every input must satisfy the relevant completeness/soundness gap. This dossier supplies ten primitive inclusions, two complement identities, two conditional collapse rules and eight regular-padding rules. Its companion JSON records all **472 ordered context pairs** incident to these endpoints. A provisional overlay with the classical and counting extensions gives 133 inclusions, 36 separations and 303 candidates. These counts must be recomputed after final integration; “candidate” is not a proof of historical openness.

The cutoff is September 1, 2026. The review separates ordinary class containments from promise-complete problems, restricted verifiers, advice and oracle results. The new Lean files contain operational definitions and supporting algebraic theorems. Their equivalence to textbook models, and the cited baseline theorems themselves, remain explicit trusted literature bridges.

## Definitions and primitive facts

**QMA(2).** `Unentangled.lean` uses two separately normalized witnesses and constructs their tensor product explicitly. The verifier cannot receive an arbitrary entangled joint witness. A proved norm-factorization identity shows that the product of normalized witnesses is normalized. The existing uniform circuit model supplies the verifier; both witness lengths and the ancillary register have polynomial bounds. Completeness is at least 2/3 and soundness at most 1/3 on every input.

Ignoring the second witness gives QMA ⊆ QMA(2). For the NEXP upper bound, guess polynomial-precision descriptions of the two exponentially long amplitude vectors, normalize approximately and simulate to constant accuracy. The constant promise gap absorbs rounding. The standard amplification and multiple-witness equivalences are proved by [Harrow–Montanaro](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0017). The upper bound and its limits are summarized in [Gharibian, §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18010v1). Neither PSPACE nor CH is inferred as an upper bound. The 2026 EXP algorithm for [StoqMA(2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27886v2) restricts destructive interference and does not apply to this endpoint.

**StoqMA.** `Stoquastic.lean` supplies concrete NOT, CNOT and Toffoli gates, explicit zero/plus ancillary preparation and an X-basis measurement of one designated output. Gates act by basis permutations; norm preservation, serialization injectivity and acceptance bounds are proved. The threshold object contains three polynomial-time computable integer functions giving a rational denominator and two numerators, together with an explicit polynomial bound on the reciprocal gap. The endpoint takes their union, with 1/2 ≤ b(n) < a(n) ≤ 1.

This matches the variable-gap convention in [Bravyi–Bessen–Terhal, Definition 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0611021). Their §4.1.1 proves StoqMA ⊆ SBP. General quantum verification also gives StoqMA ⊆ QMA. For MA ⊆ StoqMA, use a perfectly complete, amplified coherent MA verifier and a controlled swap between a zero ancilla and a plus ancilla. The resulting X-measurement accepts good witnesses with probability 1 and accepts bad inputs with probability at most 1/2 plus a negligible quantity. This construction is described in [Aharonov–Grilo–Liu, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02835v4).

The threshold union matters. It is not a claim that all allowed threshold pairs define the same class. In particular, [Liu, Corollary 24](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.TQC.2021.4) identifies the exact soundness-1/2 boundary with NP. Choosing that boundary alone would change the endpoint. Choosing soundness 1/3 would be worse: a nonnegative witness always has acceptance at least 1/2. General amplification remains an open issue; the recent [sparse-Hamiltonian completeness result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02845v1) does not settle it.

**BQL.** `Logspace.lean` gives a concrete classical transducer read-only access to the actual input x. The transducer uses logarithmic workspace and polynomial time to emit a polynomial-size quantum circuit C_x on logarithmically many qubits, initialized to zero. The output tape is write-only. Both classical control and quantum workspace have explicit bounds; the n-bit input is not copied into the counted quantum register. A polynomial-time circuit generator without the logarithmic workspace bound would admit excessive classical preprocessing.

[Le Gall–Nishimura–Yakaryılmaz, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02681v1) explicitly gives BPL ⊆ BQL ⊆ PL. [Fefferman–Remscrim, Definitions 4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03530) directly uses input-dependent space-uniform H/CNOT/T circuits with an all-zero initial state; Theorem 1 supplies the bridge from intermediate-measurement computation. Flipping the final output establishes complement closure. The polynomial clock is essential: unbounded-time bounded-error quantum logspace and postselected BQL equal PL, while that equality is not asserted for BQL here.

The 2025 [Apers–Edenhofer results](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2025.18) were checked at the level of their path hypotheses. Corollary 3 places StrongFewL in BQL. It requires polynomially many paths between every pair of configurations, which is stronger than UL's bound on accepting paths. Theorem 2 also constrains paths entering and leaving relevant endpoints. Theorem 4 supplies a total graph language by checking strong fewness, but does not prove ordinary UL ⊆ BQL, NL ⊆ BQL or BQL ⊆ SC. A common PL/NC upper bound does not supply SC's simultaneous time and space bounds.

**QSZK.** `Statistical.lean` uses the complete Quantum State Distinguishability problem. Two polynomial-time uniform samplers prepare pure states and discard explicit environment registers. Only the retained outputs are compared. The code defines their distance through the finite-dimensional variational characterization: maximize the difference in acceptance probabilities over binary projective measurements, implemented as a norm-preserving matrix followed by a subset of computational-basis outcomes. The unrestricted measurement is a mathematical definition of distance, not an efficient oracle available to either sampler.

The finite-dimensional Helstrom identity equates this quantity with half the trace norm. That identity is a trusted bridge, not a new Lean axiom. The formalization proves sampler normalization, probability bounds, identical-sampler closeness and incompatibility of the far and close predicates. Summing over environment basis states performs the partial trace at the level of measured probabilities; permitting a measurement of the environment would define the wrong problem.

The benchmark uses distance at least 2/3 on yes inputs and at most 1/3 on no inputs. [Watrous's original characterization](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0202111), the [quantum-rewinding result](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/Papers/ZeroKnowledgeAgainstQuantumAttacks.pdf), and [Vidick–Watrous, §5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.01664v1) supply completeness, polarization, complement closure and the full-verifier interpretation. To reconcile weak boundaries with strict-gap statements, use close threshold 3/8 and far threshold 5/8: 1/3 < 3/8 < (5/8)² and 5/8 < 2/3. Polarization gives slack for the reverse encoding. The total-language restriction requires both bounds on all inputs, so the definition does not treat a promise-complete problem itself as a total language.

The primitive lower bounds are BQP ⊆ QSZK and SZK ⊆ QSZK. An amplified BQP output bit can be compared with a fixed bit. For SZK, generate diagonal quantum states from the complete classical Statistical Difference problem; their trace distance is the classical total variation distance. This avoids assuming that every classical zero-knowledge protocol is automatically secure against quantum verifiers. The upper bound is QSZK ⊆ QIP(2) ⊆ PSPACE, using [Jain–Upadhyay–Watrous](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/Papers/QIP2inPSPACE.pdf). The [2026 improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11597v2) bounds the honest prover's space; it does not turn the protocol into QMA or put QSZK in PP. Swapping the two samplers leaves distance unchanged: QSZK complement closure uses its actual theorem.

## Conditional rules

The new counting endpoint makes a stronger consequence of a familiar hypothesis scoreable. If PP ⊆ QMA, then QMA = PP = coQMA. [Vinkhuijzen–Deutz, Theorem 4](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2019/131/download/) gives closure of QMA ∩ coQMA under deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions, so **PSharpP ⊆ PP**. Their Theorem 11 also gives **QMA ⊆ coQMA ⇒ PH ⊆ QMA**, after complementing the premise to get equality.

For the oracle simulation, Merlin supplies a classical query transcript and amplified witnesses for its answers. The verifier first measures the transcript, checks that the claimed queries match its deterministic computation, then verifies the answers. On a false transcript there is a wrong answer; local soundness bounds its acceptance even if the witness bundle is entangled. On a true transcript a product of good witnesses passes with high probability. This argument uses total oracle languages; no out-of-promise query needs a guessed completion.

These two rules do not infer CH ⊆ QMA. The distinction is especially easy to obscure because of the history of quantum advice: Aaronson's [2017 correction](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=3256) withdrew a counting-hierarchy consequence, and [Yirka's 2025 paper](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v021a007/) subsequently repaired it under the stronger hypothesis PP ⊆ BQP/qpoly. That advice hypothesis is not PP ⊆ QMA and is not an endpoint in this roster.

For each of the four new classes T, the dossier also supplies E ⊆ T ⇒ EXP ⊆ T and NE ⊆ T ⇒ NEXP ⊆ T. These are the existing length-regular padding argument with the following model checks:

- For QMA(2), the verifier computes the padded input and preserves the product partition of the two witnesses; substituted polynomial lengths remain polynomial.
- For StoqMA, padding is deterministic classical preprocessing, compiled reversibly. The padded length is exactly one fixed polynomial in the original length, so the rational thresholds and reciprocal-gap bound compose without an input-dependent threshold ambiguity. Zero/plus preparation and the final X measurement remain unchanged in kind.
- For QSZK, compose the padding function with both sampler generators. The output states and their distance are unchanged, and all register and time bounds remain polynomial.
- For BQL, generate the padding bits on demand using logarithmic-space counters. Simulate the logspace circuit generator without materializing the padded string on its workspace. A polynomial padded length has logarithmic address length, and repeated input scans cost only polynomial time. Quantum workspace remains logarithmic.

No arbitrary oracle relativization is used in these arguments. No new ordinary quantum separation or independence result is proposed.

## Coverage and remaining review

The JSON lists both orientations of every pair touching the four new endpoints, including all eleven new context classes and the eleven demoted background classes. Family notes cover nonuniform circuits, classical space, counting, randomness, proof systems, quantum verification, PH, exponential resources and real feasibility. Candidate interfaces include QSZK versus PP/PSharpP/CH, QMA(2) versus PSPACE/CH, BQL versus BPL/UL/SC and StoqMA versus BQP. A shared upper bound, a neighboring complete problem or a specialized verifier cannot decide these pairs.

The relevant pre-cutoff 2025–2026 papers were checked for such transfers. September 2026 oracle and perfect-completeness claims after the cutoff are excluded, without judging their validity. This is a bounded extension audit. The final baseline still needs merged closure/SAT checks, independent semantic review, and a fresh residual-risk assessment; compilation and a complete pair index alone do not establish historical correctness.
