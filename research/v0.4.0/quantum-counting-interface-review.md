Here `PSharpP` means the decision class P^#P = P^PP. It is neither the function class #P nor PP^PP. QMA2 means two unentangled polynomial-size witnesses with an inverse-polynomial gap. QIP(2) means two exchanged messages, including a challenge from the verifier. These two meanings of “two” cannot be interchanged.

## QSZK and counting

The trace-distance route still reaches PSPACE. Watrous's Corollary 10 composes circuit-to-density-matrix construction with Proposition 11, a parallel trace-norm algorithm for an **explicit matrix**. A polynomial-qubit circuit can describe a matrix of exponential dimension. The composition has polynomial depth and exponential size, and yields a polynomial-space simulation. Computing an individual entry through counting does not turn the entire spectral computation into a polynomial-time #P-oracle algorithm. Neither PP, PSharpP, nor a fixed counting-hierarchy level follows from that proof. [Watrous 2002, Corollary 10 and Proposition 11, printed pp. 15–16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0202111).

The interactive route has the same endpoint: QSZK lies in QIP(2) and its complement, and the two-message class lies in PSPACE. The verifier first sends a quantum state; the prover applies a channel to the received register. Jain–Upadhyay–Watrous optimize this channel using an exponentially large semidefinite program. Replacing the channel by a static proof, or applying a bound for two independent proofs, changes the model. [Jain–Upadhyay–Watrous 2009, §§2.2–4 and the main theorem](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/Papers/QIP2inPSPACE.pdf).

The June 2026 revision of Le Gall–Liu–Wang sharpens the honest prover's space usage to linear in the transcript size. Its Holevo–Helstrom and Uhlmann procedures still take single-exponential time in the number of qubits. Theorem 3.4 and Theorem 4.4 retain interaction; §1.4 explicitly asks for a better classical upper bound than PSPACE. This is useful contemporary evidence about the chain's scope, not a certification that every total-language comparison is open. [Le Gall–Liu–Wang, v2, 28 June 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11597v2).

Two oracle results guard against easy transfers in opposite directions. Bouland et al., Theorem 1.1, give an oracle separating NISZK from unbounded-error computation, hence a relativized SZK/QSZK-versus-PP obstruction. Menda–Watrous, Theorem 4, give an oracle with UP ∩ coUP outside QSZK, hence a relativized PP-versus-QSZK obstruction. Neither is an ordinary separation. The first also does not establish QSZK outside PSharpP or CH merely because PP is contained in those larger classes. [Bouland et al., v4, §1.1 and Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.02888v4); [Menda–Watrous 2018, §4.1, Theorem 4](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/Papers/OraclesQSZK.pdf).

PostBQP = PP allows conditioning on an event of possibly exponentially small probability. It does not remove that resource from a simulation or supply a QSZK protocol for PP. Similarly, the repaired 2025 implication PP ⊆ BQP/qpoly ⇒ CH = QMA is conditional and uses quantum advice. It supplies no unconditional comparison among these endpoints. [Aaronson, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0412187); [Yirka 2025, Theorem 3.3](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v021a007/v021a007.pdf).

## QMA2, PSPACE, and CH

The counting-hierarchy route has an explicit missing premise. Gharibian et al. prove QCPH ⊆ P^(PP^PP), but QCPH quantifies over classical strings. Their Corollary 1.3 obtains the same upper bound for QMA2 **if QCPH = QPH**. Their mixed-state second-level/refereed-game PSPACE bound does not, by itself, collapse the quantum hierarchy. [Gharibian et al., v2, Theorem 1.2 and Corollaries 1.3, 1.9–1.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.11139v2).

The October 2025 result published at ICALP 2026 is particularly easy to overextend. Grewal–Rudolph prove QMA2 ⊆ pureQΣ₂ ⊆ mixedQΣ₃. They also prove equality of the **unions** pureQPH and QPH, using a transformation that increases the number of alternations. This does not identify pureQΣ₂ with mixedQΣ₂. Therefore it cannot be combined with the mixed second-level PSPACE bound to conclude QMA2 ⊆ PSPACE, nor with the classical-proof hierarchy's counting bound to conclude QMA2 ⊆ CH. [Grewal–Rudolph, Theorems 1.1–1.2, Corollary 1.3, and §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06522v1).

Two other PSPACE results apply to restricted problems. Kamminga–Rudolph's PureSuperQMA upper bound uses polynomially many quadratic constraints; their embedding into QMA2 does not prove the reverse inclusion. Shi–Wu's Corollary 2 restricts a QMA2 verifier to logarithmically many gates crossing the two proof registers. Neither restriction is available for an arbitrary QMA2 protocol. [Kamminga–Rudolph, v2, Proposition 1.6, Theorem 1.9, and §6; ITCS 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03096v2); [Shi–Wu, Corollary 2 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.0808).

Amplification also requires care. Harrow–Montanaro amplify ordinary QMA2 while increasing witness size. This does not establish the earlier, stronger witness-size-preserving amplification conjecture. The published Aaronson–Beigi–Drucker–Fefferman–Shor paper explains that its earlier conditional PSPACE argument was removed: that conjecture would actually imply QMA2 = QMA. A superseded conference statement cannot be combined with later amplification under a different resource convention. [Harrow–Montanaro, §3, Theorem 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0017); [Aaronson et al. 2009, introduction, printed pp. 3–4](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v005a001/v005a001.pdf).

Nonnegative witnesses provide another different model. Jeronimo–Wu's NEXP characterization constrains both honest and dishonest witnesses to nonnegative amplitudes; its sufficiently-large-gap conversion is conditional. Miyamoto's August 2026 **Theorem 1** reaches completeness 1−1/p and soundness 1/4+1/p. **Corollary 2 is a one-way inclusion** into that regime, despite a broader equality in the abstract. Proposition 6 converts soundness to min(1,4s) for real witnesses and min(1,8s) for complex witnesses. Both bounds become 1 at Theorem 1's parameters. Thus the formal statements do not establish ordinary QMA2 = NEXP or PSPACE ⊆ QMA2. The real/complex distinction must also remain explicit. [Jeronimo–Wu, Theorem 1.4, Corollary 1.5, and §8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18790v1); [Miyamoto, Theorem 1, Corollaries 2–3, and Proposition 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07986v1).

Postselection and interaction yield further NEXP characterizations with additional resources. Kinoshita's postQMA2 conditions on a postselection event. Grewal–Kretschmer's QIP_unent[3] uses an adaptive exchange of messages. Ordinary QMA2 can embed into the latter by ignoring a round; its NEXP upper bound cannot be reversed. [Kinoshita 2018, Definition 2 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09732); [Grewal–Kretschmer 2025, Theorem 1.1 and its protocol](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/145/download/).

## StoqMA and BQP

The existing chains MA ⊆ StoqMA ⊆ SBP ∩ QMA and BQP ⊆ QMA do not compare StoqMA with BQP. The StoqMA verifier uses reversible classical gates, zero/plus ancillas, and a final X-basis measurement. Its threshold union matters: a general inverse-polynomial gap cannot be replaced by perfect completeness or arbitrary standard error parameters. The near-perfect-completeness MA bound and its amplification consequence retain their hypotheses. [Bravyi–Bessen–Terhal, Definition 4 and §4.1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0611021); [Aharonov–Grilo–Liu, Corollaries 1.4–1.5 and Remark 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02835v4).

A genuine May 2026 update improves an intermediate upper bound: Gay–Jeronimo prove StoqMA(k) = StoqMA for polynomially many provers. Their Theorem 5.1 constructs a one-witness verifier with gap 11Δ/32. The positive de Finetti theorem applies to **entrywise nonnegative acceptance matrices**. General QMA2 does not have this property; nonnegative-witness QMA+ can still use interference in its verifier. Thus this collapse does not establish QMA2 ⊆ PP, or either comparison with BQP. It supersedes the earlier general EXP bound for StoqMA(k) as a class containment. [Gay–Jeronimo, 15 May 2026, Theorems 3.2 and 5.1, Corollaries 5.1–6.2, and §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16249v1).

Liu–Wu's July revision separately proves StoqMA with logarithmic-size witnesses equals BPP, and identifies precise-gap variants with PSPACE or NEXP. These results respectively restrict witness size or permit exponentially small gaps. Their unrestricted stoquastic algorithms are not BQP algorithms for polynomial-size witnesses. Grilo–Rozos's May result proves sparse stoquastic Hamiltonian completeness, not a simulation by BQP. [Liu–Wu, Theorems 6.1–6.2, 7.1–7.2, 8.2, and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27886v2); [Grilo–Rozos, Theorems 1.1–1.2 and §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02845v1).

Recent Hamiltonian algorithms do not cover the complete problem. The July Lee–Yang/Heisenberg results give quantum algorithms for specified Hamiltonian families, including bipartite Quantum Max-Cut. They do not give a reduction of arbitrary StoqMA instances to those families. The VGP result instead enlarges the family of StoqMA-complete energy problems; its PSPACE-complete **recognition** task is a different problem from energy estimation under a VGP promise. [Rayudu–Takahashi, Theorem 11 and §1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10765v1); [Bravyi–Gosset–Liu–Wong, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14401v1); [Karakashian–Hen, Theorems 4 and 6 and the recognition section](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18596v1).

Two simulation claims need additional distinctions. Positive tensor-network contraction has a BPP algorithm for additive error scaled by a product of matrix 1-norms, while its StoqMA hardness uses multiplicative error. The error measures cannot be exchanged without a bound on their ratio. The February 2026 guided-stoquastic lower bound concerns sampling with an adjacency-list oracle and a supplied guiding state. It is neither an ordinary decision separation nor a BQP-hardness theorem for stoquastic energy estimation. [Jiang et al., Theorems 3–4, formal Theorems 27 and 31](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05414v1); [Hamoudi–Le Borgne–Sridhara, Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.2, and §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23183v1).

## What this establishes

The primary statements and the proof interfaces needed for these transfers were checked; this is not a line-by-line re-proof of every cited result. Inclusions proved for promise classes can be restricted to total languages when their reductions preserve the promise on every input. A promise separation does not automatically produce a separating total language. Query, sampling, advice, postselection, and gap restrictions were retained throughout. No canonical facts or rules are proposed.

This pass addresses the old-quantum-interface and recent-literature portions of the residual ledger. Its most useful additions are the pure/mixed hierarchy level distinction, the May stoquastic collapse, and the formal-versus-abstract scope of the August amplification claim. They strengthen the record of excluded transfers but do not provide a calibrated probability of an undiscovered theorem. The broad working estimate remains 2.9375 expected incorrectly classified pairs, with the original limitations and sensitivity range. QSZK's exact Lean-to-literature bridge and real-feasibility semantics are being reviewed separately; this dossier does not claim to discharge those obligations.
